## How we work on each paragraph

1. **Transcribe.** Copy the homework bullets and the paragraph exactly as written, including spelling and grammar mistakes. Mark any word we cannot read as `[unclear]`. No fixing at this step.
2. **Polish.** Make it easy to say out loud and fit the recipe below.
3. **Assemble.** Once all five are polished, join them in speaking order and check the hand-offs and the total time.

### Polish rules (the kids' work should shine)

- Keep the kid's own ideas, examples, and favourite phrases. If a sentence already works, leave it alone.
- Fix spelling, grammar, and run-on sentences. Split long sentences so they are easy to say in one breath.
- Reorder to fit the recipe: hook, say-and-show, hand-off.
- Do not add facts the kid did not write. If something is missing (a hook, a prop, a hand-off), write it under "Ask the kid" instead of inventing it.
- Every change is listed under "What changed" so the kid can see it and say yes or no.

## Speaking order

Rossy (Identify) → Jason (Design) → Bowen (Create) → Jonathan (Iterate) → Jasper (Communicate)

Each speaker hands off to the next speaker by name.

## Paragraph recipe

| Step | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Hook** | One sentence that makes people curious: a question, a surprising fact, or a mini story. |
| **Say, then show** | The homework answers, one or two sentences each. Say the big idea, say why it matters, then hold up or point to something: the piezo sensor, the M5Stack screen, the test wood. |
| **Hand-off** | The key message in one sentence, then introduce the next speaker by name and what they will talk about. Not a thank-you. |

Checklist for each finished paragraph:

- Starts with a hook
- Uses all the homework answers
- Has one "Imagine..." detail and something to show
- Ends with a hand-off, not a thank-you
- 45 to 60 seconds read out loud

Time check: five speakers at 60 seconds is exactly five minutes, with no room for hand-offs or the demo. Aim for about 50 seconds each (roughly 100 to 110 words).
