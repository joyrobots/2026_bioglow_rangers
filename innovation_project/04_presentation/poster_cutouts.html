Meet the beetle
The emerald ash borer (EAB) is an invasive beetle from Asia.
What it does
Its larvae chew tunnels under the bark.
Why it is hard to find
Nobody can see the larvae from the outside.
Trees we lost
TRCA has cut down over 45,000 ash trees.
Ash trees today
Mature ash trees are almost all gone across the GTA.
Our problem
How can we find the larvae early?
When it arrived
The EAB was first found in Ontario in 2002.
How people check today
People peel bark or cut branches to look for tunnels.

The experts
TRCA, York Region, TreeAzin, and LEAF.
What we learned
After 30% of the leaves are gone, it is too late for medicine.
Why early matters
Finding the EAB early gives a tree the best chance.
Our field trip
We explored Bruce's Mill Conservation Area and found samples.
The medicine
TreeAzin is made from neem tree seeds and is injected into the trunk.
What foresters want
Foresters want tools that find larvae before the leaves change.
Saving seeds
TRCA saved a couple of hundred mature ash trees for their seeds.
Trees still treated
York Region plans to treat about 31 ash trees in 2027.
