## Toronto and Region Conservation Authority (TRCA)

**Expert:** Andrew Chaisson, ISA Certified Arborist & Technologist

### How is EAB prevented from destroying ash trees?

- Chemical treatments like TreeAzin are used to protect individual trees. TreeAzin is an organic insecticide made from neem tree seeds, developed by Bioforest with the Canadian Forest Service.
- TreeAzin acts as a growth regulator. It stops larvae from molting, which kills them, and it makes female EAB less fertile, so fewer eggs are laid and fewer hatch.
- Unlike broad-spectrum insecticides, TreeAzin is targeted. It limits EAB feeding without harming other insects.

### How is EAB detected early, and why is it so challenging?

- Adult exit holes are tiny and hard to spot when there are only a few. A lot of larval feeding happens long before crown decline can be seen.
- In the past, early detection relied on wildlife (woodpeckers and squirrels feeding on larvae) or on invasive methods: branch sampling, and peeling "bark windows" to look at the cambium layer for winding feeding galleries.
- Early detection technologies, like acoustic sensors, are vital. They can monitor the leading edges of infestation across North America, detect second waves that threaten regenerating saplings, and give a framework for future forest pests.

### Why do EAB specifically attack ash trees, and why is the damage so severe in North America?

- EAB is host-specific. It feeds almost only on trees in the ash genus (*Fraxinus*) and closely related species.
- In EAB's native range (temperate and northeastern Asia), the native ash species evolved resistance alongside it, and natural predators keep EAB numbers low. North American ash trees do not have this resistance, and local wildlife only feed on EAB when they happen to discover it.
- Milder winters caused by climate change help more EAB eggs and larvae survive the winter inside trees.

### What is the scale of infestation and tree loss in the region?

- Almost no ash tree larger than 10 to 15 cm in trunk diameter remains uninfested, unless it is regularly treated with TreeAzin.
- During peak infestation, foresters inventoried 50 to 150 infested trees per day.
- TRCA has cut over 45,000 hazard ash trees for public safety. It has preserved a couple of hundred mature trees (black, green, white, and blue ash) to keep canopy cover and a future seed source.

### Why do larvae live inside the trunk?

- The cambium layer gives larvae the nutrients they need to develop through 4 growth stages before they pupate into adult beetles.
- Living inside the trunk insulates larvae from extreme winter cold and shields them from predators that cannot dig through bark.

## Regional Municipality of York (Urban Forestry)

**Expert:** Julia Reale, Area Forester

### Do foresters track regenerating young ash saplings in regional forests?

- Individual saplings are not inventoried or tracked. Foresters do keep an inventory of larger, healthy mature ash trees, to preserve seed sources for future generations.
- York Region planned TreeAzin treatments for about 12 ash trees in the York Regional Forest and 19 along regional roadways for 2027.

### How do foresters choose between treating an ash tree or removing it?

- The decision depends on the tree's overall health and on public safety. TreeAzin is injected directly into the trunk every two years.
- Trees with about 30% or more canopy loss are generally no longer candidates for treatment, because their vascular system is too damaged to transport the medicine.
- Trees that are a safety hazard along trails or roadways are marked for removal.

### What key information do foresters wish they had before leaf loss occurs?

- Foresters want to identify naturally resistant "lingering ash" trees that survive in heavily infested forests, so their seeds can be collected to restore resistant ash populations.
- Reliable, non-destructive tools (such as acoustic or vibration sensors) that detect larvae before crown damage occurs are highly valued for municipal field triage.

## Lallemand Plant Care (TreeAzin team)

**Expert:** Adriana Kairiss, Marketing Manager

### How healthy must a tree's vascular system be for TreeAzin to work?

- TreeAzin relies on intact vascular "straws" (xylem and phloem) to move up to the leaves.
- Once 30% or more of the crown has thinned or died back, the tree cannot move the treatment effectively. At that point, removal and replacement is safer.

### Is treatment more effective when given to a completely healthy tree?

- Yes. Early, preventative protection gives trees the best chance of survival.
- EAB larvae feed hidden under the bark for years before visible symptoms appear, so proactive monitoring (for example, green prism traps) and early detection are critical.

### Can sick trees be mistakenly injected?

- Yes, because the internal transport systems can be severely damaged while the canopy still looks green.
- A professional assessment of the crown, the trunk, and the signs of infestation is required before injection.

## LEAF (Local Enhancement and Appreciation of Forests)

**Expert:** Natasha Jovanovic, Senior Education Coordinator

### What is the current status of mature ash trees across the GTA?

- Mature ash trees have been almost completely wiped out across the Greater Toronto Area (GTA).

### What makes early detection so hard?

- The main difficulty for arborists is that larval feeding cuts the internal transport systems under the bark long before the leaves show visible thinning or yellowing.
